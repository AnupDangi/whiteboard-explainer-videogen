# Conditional probability and Bayes theorem

[S1] Conditional probability restricts to cases where B happened: P(A|B) = count(A and B)/count(B). [S2] Bayes flips the condition using prior, likelihood, and normalizer: P(D|+) = P(+|D)P(D)/P(+). [S3] Example: 1% disease, 90% true-positive, 9% false-positive gives about 9% posterior after one positive. [S4] Base rate dominates: rare conditions stay unlikely after one imperfect test.
