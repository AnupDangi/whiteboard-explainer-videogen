# Newton's second law

Newton's second law states that the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration, F = ma. Force is measured in newtons, mass in kilograms and acceleration in metres per second squared. One newton is the force that gives a one kilogram mass an acceleration of one metre per second squared.

Acceleration points in the same direction as the net force. If several forces act, add them as vectors first. When the net force is zero, acceleration is zero, so the object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity.

For a fixed force, a larger mass gives a smaller acceleration. Pushing a 2 kilogram cart with a net force of 10 newtons gives an acceleration of 5 metres per second squared. The same push on a 5 kilogram cart gives 2 metres per second squared.
