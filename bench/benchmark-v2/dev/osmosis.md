# Osmosis

Osmosis is the net movement of water across a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration. The membrane lets water pass but blocks the solute. Water moves in both directions, but more water crosses toward the side with more solute, so the water level rises there.

Water potential describes this tendency. Pure water has the highest water potential, and adding solute lowers it. Water moves from higher to lower water potential. Movement stops at equilibrium, when both sides have equal water potential, or when pressure from a stretched cell wall balances it.

A red blood cell in a hypotonic solution, which has less solute than the cell, takes in water and may burst. In a hypertonic solution, which has more solute, it loses water and shrinks. In an isotonic solution there is no net movement and the cell keeps its shape.
