# Why ocean tides occur

Tides are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the Moon, with a smaller contribution from the Sun. The Moon pulls most strongly on the side of Earth nearest to it and least strongly on the far side. This difference in pull, the tidal force, stretches the ocean into two bulges: one facing the Moon and one on the opposite side of Earth.

As Earth rotates once a day, a coastal location passes through both bulges, so most coasts have two high tides and two low tides about every 24 hours and 50 minutes. The extra 50 minutes arise because the Moon moves along its orbit while Earth rotates.

When the Sun, Earth and Moon line up, at new moon and full moon, the Sun's pull adds to the Moon's, producing larger spring tides. At the quarter moons the pulls partly cancel, producing smaller neap tides.
