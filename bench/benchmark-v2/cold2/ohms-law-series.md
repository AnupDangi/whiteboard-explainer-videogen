# Ohm's law and series circuits

Ohm's law says the current through a conductor equals the voltage across it divided by its resistance, I = V / R. Current is measured in amperes, voltage in volts and resistance in ohms. For a fixed voltage, doubling the resistance halves the current.

In a series circuit, components are connected end to end, so the same current flows through each one. The total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances, R_total = R1 + R2 + R3. The battery voltage divides among the components, and each one's voltage is its current times its resistance. The voltage drops add up to the battery voltage.

Example: a 12 volt battery drives a 4 ohm resistor and an 8 ohm resistor in series. The total resistance is 12 ohms, so the current is 1 ampere. The voltage drops are 4 volts and 8 volts, which sum to 12 volts.
