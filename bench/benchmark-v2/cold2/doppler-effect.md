# The Doppler effect

The Doppler effect is the change in the observed frequency of a wave when the source and the observer move relative to each other. When a source moves toward an observer, each wave crest is emitted closer to the previous one, so the waves are compressed and the observed frequency is higher. When the source moves away, the waves are stretched and the frequency is lower.

For sound from a moving source and a stationary observer, the observed frequency is f' = f * v / (v - vs) when approaching, and f' = f * v / (v + vs) when receding. Here f is the emitted frequency, v the speed of sound and vs the speed of the source.

An ambulance siren sounds higher as it approaches and lower after it passes. Light shows the same effect: light from receding galaxies is shifted toward red, called redshift.
