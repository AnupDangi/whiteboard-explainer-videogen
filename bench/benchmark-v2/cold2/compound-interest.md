# Compound interest

Compound interest is interest earned on both the original amount and on interest already added. With principal P, annual rate r written as a decimal, and n years of annual compounding, the balance is A = P * (1 + r)^n.

Each year the balance is multiplied by the same factor 1 + r. With P = 1000 and r = 0.05, the balance after one year is 1050. After two years it is 1050 * 1.05 = 1102.50. Simple interest would add only 50 each year, giving 1100 after two years. The extra 2.50 is interest earned on interest.

The longer the time, the larger the gap between compound and simple interest. Compounding more often within a year, such as monthly, raises the balance slightly more.
