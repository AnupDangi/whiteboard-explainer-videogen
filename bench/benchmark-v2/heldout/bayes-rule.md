# Bayes' rule with a diagnostic test

Bayes' rule updates a probability after new evidence. It states P(A given B) = P(B given A) * P(A) / P(B). P(A) is the prior, the probability of A before seeing B. P(A given B) is the posterior.

Suppose a disease affects 1 in 100 people, so the prior is 1 percent. A test detects the disease in 90 percent of people who have it, the sensitivity. It wrongly signals the disease in 9 percent of healthy people, the false positive rate. A person tests positive. What is the probability of disease?

Imagine 1000 people. 10 have the disease, and the test catches 9 of them. 990 are healthy, and the test wrongly flags about 89 of them. So 9 + 89 = 98 people test positive, and only 9 of them are sick. The posterior is 9 / 98, about 9 percent. Because the disease is rare, most positive results are false alarms.
