# Supply and demand equilibrium

In a market, the demand curve shows how much buyers want at each price. It slopes downward: as the price rises, the quantity demanded falls. The supply curve shows how much sellers offer at each price. It slopes upward: as the price rises, the quantity supplied rises.

The equilibrium price is where the two curves cross. At this price the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, so the market clears. If the price is above equilibrium, sellers offer more than buyers want, creating a surplus, and sellers cut prices. If the price is below equilibrium, buyers want more than sellers offer, creating a shortage, and the price rises.

A shift in a curve moves the equilibrium. If a new technology lowers production costs, supply shifts right, and the equilibrium price falls while the equilibrium quantity rises. If consumer income rises for a normal good, demand shifts right, and both equilibrium price and quantity rise.
