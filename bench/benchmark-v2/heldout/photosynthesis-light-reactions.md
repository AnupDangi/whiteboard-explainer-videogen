# The light reactions of photosynthesis

The light reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Photosystem II absorbs light, which excites electrons in chlorophyll. The excited electrons pass to an electron transport chain. Photosystem II replaces its lost electrons by splitting water, which releases oxygen gas and hydrogen ions into the thylakoid space.

As electrons move down the chain, protons are pumped into the thylakoid space, creating a proton gradient. Protons flow back out through ATP synthase, which makes ATP from ADP and phosphate. This is called photophosphorylation.

Electrons then reach Photosystem I, which absorbs more light and raises them to a higher energy. They are passed to NADP+ reductase, which forms NADPH. The ATP and NADPH made in the light reactions power the Calvin cycle, which builds sugar from carbon dioxide.
